# category: writing | Writing & Communication
agents: copywriter, content-strategist, translator, technical-writer

## blog-post
desc: Write a well-structured blog post with hook, clear sections, examples and SEO basics. Use for content marketing or thought leadership.
- Define audience, angle and key takeaway.
- Outline with headings.
- Write a strong hook intro.
- Use examples and data.
- End with summary and CTA.
check: One clear takeaway; scannable headings
out: Blog post draft.

## email-writer
desc: Write clear professional emails: requests, follow-ups, updates, apologies and negotiations. Use for any important email.
- Identify the goal and recipient.
- Put the ask in the first lines.
- Give context briefly.
- Make next steps explicit.
- Match tone to relationship.
check: Ask is clear; concise
out: Email draft.

## rewrite-clarity
desc: Rewrite text to be clearer, shorter and easier to read while keeping meaning. Use to improve any draft.
- Identify main point.
- Cut filler and redundancy.
- Prefer active voice and short sentences.
- Restructure for flow.
- Keep the author's voice.
check: Meaning preserved; shorter
out: Rewritten text.

## translate-localize
desc: Translate and localise text between languages (e.g. Portuguese, English, Spanish) preserving tone, idioms and formatting. Use for multilingual content.
- Identify audience and locale.
- Translate meaning, not words.
- Adapt idioms, units and formats.
- Keep placeholders and markup intact.
- Note ambiguous terms.
check: Natural in target language; formatting intact
out: Translation and notes.

## summarize-document
desc: Summarise long documents, reports, transcripts or threads into key points, decisions and action items. Use to digest content quickly.
- Identify document type and reader need.
- Extract main points.
- List decisions and action items.
- Keep important numbers.
- Offer a one-line TL;DR.
check: No invented facts; numbers preserved
out: Summary.

## proofread
desc: Proofread text for grammar, spelling, punctuation, consistency and style. Use before publishing anything.
- Check spelling and grammar.
- Check punctuation and consistency of terms.
- Check facts and names.
- Flag awkward sentences.
- List changes.
check: Changes listed; meaning untouched
out: Corrected text and change list.

## press-release
desc: Write a press release with headline, dateline, lead, quotes, boilerplate and contact. Use for announcements.
- Write a newsworthy headline.
- Answer who, what, when, where, why in the lead.
- Add quotes (marked as drafts for approval).
- Add boilerplate and contact.
- Keep to one page.
check: Quotes marked for approval; facts verified
out: Press release draft.

## speech-script
desc: Write speeches, talk scripts or video scripts with structure, timing and delivery cues. Use for presentations or videos.
- Define audience, length and goal.
- Structure: hook, story, points, close.
- Write for the ear: short sentences.
- Add timing marks.
- Add delivery cues.
check: Fits time; spoken-friendly
out: Script.

## newsletter
desc: Write a newsletter issue with sections, curated links, commentary and a CTA. Use for regular audience updates.
- Pick theme and highlights.
- Write intro.
- Add sections with commentary.
- Add CTA.
- Write subject line variants.
check: Scannable; one CTA
out: Newsletter draft.

## tone-adapter
desc: Adapt a text to a different tone or audience (formal, casual, technical, executive, friendly) without changing facts. Use when the same message must reach different readers.
- Identify target audience and tone.
- Adjust vocabulary and sentence length.
- Adjust detail level.
- Keep facts identical.
- Show side-by-side if useful.
check: Facts unchanged; tone consistent
out: Adapted text.

# category: research | Research & Analysis
agents: research-analyst, market-researcher

## deep-research
desc: Research a question in depth using multiple sources, compare evidence and produce a cited report with confidence levels. Use for complex questions.
- Break the question into sub-questions.
- Search each one; prefer primary sources.
- Extract claims with citations and dates.
- Compare and resolve conflicts.
- Write findings with confidence and gaps.
check: Every claim cited; conflicts noted
out: Research report.

## fact-check
desc: Verify claims against reliable sources and rate them true, false, misleading or unverified. Use before publishing or deciding.
- List each factual claim.
- Find primary sources.
- Compare and rate.
- Note context.
- Cite sources.
check: Primary sources used; ratings justified
out: Fact-check table.

## literature-review
desc: Review academic or technical literature: search, screen, summarise and synthesise findings. Use for research projects.
- Define scope and search terms.
- Search databases (Google Scholar, arXiv, PubMed).
- Screen for relevance and quality.
- Summarise each source.
- Synthesise themes and gaps.
check: Search terms documented; quality assessed
out: Literature review.

## market-sizing
desc: Estimate TAM, SAM and SOM with top-down and bottom-up methods and explicit assumptions. Use for business plans and pitches.
- Define the market and customer.
- Top-down from industry data.
- Bottom-up from customers × price.
- Reconcile.
- List assumptions with sources.
check: Both methods; assumptions sourced
out: Market sizing table.

## competitor-teardown
desc: Analyse a single competitor deeply: product, pricing, positioning, funnel, tech and weaknesses. Use to understand a rival.
- Collect public information.
- Analyse product and UX.
- Analyse pricing and packaging.
- Analyse marketing and positioning.
- Summarise strengths and weaknesses.
check: Public sources only; cited
out: Teardown report.

## technology-comparison
desc: Compare technologies, tools or vendors on features, performance, cost, maturity and fit with sources. Use for tool selection.
- Define criteria.
- Gather data from docs and benchmarks.
- Build comparison table.
- Test critical claims if possible.
- Recommend with context.
check: Sources dated; criteria weighted
out: Comparison table and recommendation.

## trend-analysis
desc: Identify and assess trends in an industry or technology with evidence and implications. Use for strategy.
- Collect signals from news, reports and data.
- Group into trends.
- Assess momentum and maturity.
- Analyse implications.
- Recommend actions.
check: Evidence for each trend
out: Trend report.

## interview-synthesis
desc: Synthesise interview notes or transcripts into themes, quotes and insights. Use after customer or expert interviews.
- Tag notes by topic.
- Cluster into themes.
- Count frequency.
- Select quotes.
- Derive insights.
check: Quotes attributed anonymously; themes counted
out: Synthesis report.

## swot-analysis
desc: Build a SWOT analysis with evidence and turn it into strategic actions. Use for strategy planning.
- List internal strengths and weaknesses.
- List external opportunities and threats.
- Support each with evidence.
- Derive SO/WO/ST/WT strategies.
- Prioritise actions.
check: Evidence-based; actions derived
out: SWOT and actions.

## source-evaluation
desc: Evaluate the credibility of sources: authority, methodology, bias, recency and corroboration. Use when sources conflict.
- Identify author and publisher.
- Assess methodology.
- Check bias and funding.
- Check recency.
- Check corroboration.
check: Each criterion rated
out: Source credibility table.
